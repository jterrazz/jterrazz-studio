# Content & reach

Reach = (content worth citing) × (places it's seen). Most sites fail on the second factor: publishing is the midpoint, not the finish line.

## What to write (in order of leverage)

1. **Refresh before create.** Pages that had traffic and lost it are the cheapest wins: update facts, deepen, honestly re-date. Check Search Console for decaying queries first.
2. **Comparison & alternative content** — the most-cited format by both humans and LLMs (~1/3 of AI citations). "X vs Y", "alternatives to X", honest trade-offs.
3. **Original research / first-hand data** — numbers nobody else has (benchmarks, experiments, build logs with metrics). This is what earns links and citations passively.
4. **Definitive guides** on topics you can genuinely out-cover competitors on.

Pick topics by **search intent**, not by what you feel like writing: someone must be asking, and the page must answer *that*, not adjacent musings. Group into clusters — one pillar page, spokes for subtopics, dense internal links both ways.

## Production standards

- Differentiate with **experience**: real screenshots, real numbers, real failures. Generic coverage of a covered topic is dead weight (and drags the whole site's quality signals down).
- Every piece: identified author, date, sources for claims, one clear primary keyword/question.
- Quality over cadence. One strong piece a month beats four thin ones — thin content is negative-sum site-wide.

## Distribution loop (per piece, not per site)

For every significant piece, work down this list:

1. **Owned** — internal links from related pages + homepage/pillar, RSS feed, newsletter.
2. **Repurpose** — the core insight as a standalone X/LinkedIn post (native format, not a bare link), a thread for the process/numbers.
3. **Communities** — share where the topic genuinely lives (relevant subreddits, HN for engineering write-ups, niche Discords/forums) with the insight up front. Communities smell self-promotion; lead with value, disclose authorship.
4. **Products**: submit to directories and showcases (Product Hunt, alternative-to listings, awesome-lists, App Store features via in-app events).
5. **Earned** — pitch original data to newsletters/roundups that cover the niche; answer relevant questions linking the piece only when it truly answers.

Third-party presence compounds: profiles (GitHub, X, LinkedIn) consistent and linked (`sameAs`), authored guest content, being the cited source in others' posts. This is also the GEO "presence" pillar — LLMs cite the ecosystem around you as much as your domain.

## Measure

- Search Console: queries gained/lost per page, quarterly decay review.
- Analytics: referral sources per piece — double down on channels that actually send readers.
- Per-piece retro after ~4 weeks: kept, grew, or flatlined? Feed the answer into the next topic choice.
